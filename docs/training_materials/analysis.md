# Handbook Analysis: Drills & Exercises — Technical Free Skiing and Gate Training

---

## 1. Handbook Overview

**Title:** Drills and Exercises — Technical Free Skiing and Gate Training
**Publisher:** Alpine Canada Alpin (ACA), National Coaching Certification Program (NCCP)
**Year:** 2016
**Audience:** NCCP-certified alpine ski racing coaches

**Sport/Discipline:** Alpine ski racing (slalom, giant slalom, super-G). This is a ski *racing* handbook, not a general recreational skiing guide. All drills are oriented toward technical free-skiing as preparation for gate training and competition.

**Structure (108 pages):**
1. Introduction (pp. 4–6) — Philosophy of drills/exercises as skill-isolation tools, whole-part-whole coaching model
2. Technical Free Skiing — Quick Reference Guides (pp. 7–11) — Drills catalogued by skill area across four levels
3. Technical Free Skiing — Drill Descriptions (pp. 12–67) — 40+ individual drills with description, focus, terrain, turn shape, variations, and coaching notes
4. Gate Training — Quick Reference Guides (pp. 68–73) — Gate drills catalogued by skill area across four levels
5. Gate Training — Drill Descriptions (pp. 74–107) — 20+ gate-specific drills and course types
6. Concluding Remarks — AIM 2 WIN (p. 108) — Sequencing principles

**Four Athlete Levels Used Throughout:**

| Handbook Level | Age Brackets | Coaching Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Entry Level | U8, U10, U12 | Learn to Train |
| Development Level | U14, U16 | Learn to Train to Learn to Race |
| Performance Level | U18, U21 | Learn to Race to Train to Race |
| High Performance | U21, Senior | Train to Race to Train to Win |

## 2. Drills & Exercises Found

### Technical Free Skiing Drills (pp. 12–67)

**1. Thousand Steps**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Skeleton alignment, forward movement off a stable outside ski, active lateral/forward/vertical movements
- Key steps: Active lateral and forward steps off outside ski through the entire arc; shoulders and elbows ahead of kneecaps; upper body stays stable
- Image: `p012_01.png`

**2. Hoppsving**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Vertical movement, alignment with all joints, balance over outside ski
- Key steps: Two-footed hops off centre of skis around entire arc; remain stable over outside ski despite hopping off both skis; move off balls of feet; maintain athletic upper body position
- Image: `p013_01.png`

**3. Hands-All-Over (4 variations)**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Upper body stability, dynamic leg mobility, upper/lower body separation
- Key steps (basic): Hands on hips with fingertips on hip flexors; perform fall-line turns minimising pressure on fingertips; stand tall and use all leg joints
- Variations: (a) Hands on hips, (b) Cossack — arms crossed, elbows at shoulder height pressed forward, (c) Hands behind head — fingers interlaced behind neck with half-crunch core engagement, (d) Hands above head
- Images: `p015_01.png`, `p016_01.png`, `p017_01.png`

**4. Horizontal Pole**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Stability over outside ski, alignment throughout turn
- Key steps: Hold full-length bamboo at eye level with elbows outstretched; keep pole level throughout arc; optionally tilt inside end higher to promote lateral mobility
- Terrain: Moderate to steep

**5. Handle Bar**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Core use for upper/lower body separation, consistent arm/hand home position, upper body discipline
- Key steps: Hold 18-inch curved bar fitted with ski handles at chest level; maintain forward hand and arm position; leg movements drive the turn
- Image: `p021_01.png`

**6. No Poles**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Disciplined arm/hand position, upper body rotational stability, core isolation
- Key steps: Arms in front, elbows at chest level; maintain consistent hand width; initiate movements with feet and legs

**7. Hip Lift**
- Difficulty: Entry to Performance
- Purpose: Alignment using all joints, forward/backward movement adjustment
- Key steps: Poles placed together behind back just under the bum; perform turns, poles lifted and pulled forward to "lift" hips; shifts weight more forward over base of support

**8. Posture Exercise**
- Difficulty: Entry to Performance
- Purpose: Upper body discipline, forward/backward movement adjustment, alignment
- Key steps: Poles behind back, elbows hooked around poles to bring shoulders back and chest up; turns performed with aligned posture; initiate with feet and legs
- Note: Do not use if athlete has shoulder problems

**9. Victory Exercise**
- Difficulty: Entry to Performance
- Purpose: Global movement adjustment, alignment with all joints, upper/lower body separation
- Key steps: Hold poles as wide as possible above head in a V; reach high with weight on balls of feet; maintain hip/knee/ankle alignment; optionally lift uphill hand higher to promote lateral mobility

**10. Goal Post (Window)**
- Difficulty: Development to Performance
- Purpose: Upper/lower body separation; external focus of attention
- Key steps: Hold poles at shoulder height baskets pointing to sky, arms wider than shoulder width; visually frame a specific external point between the poles; ski toward that point without losing it from view

**11. Fall Line Skating**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Stability on outside ski, global movements from a loaded ski, centre of mass in line of travel
- Key steps: Skate hockey-style straight down the fall line; maintain general form; move centre of mass in the line of travel

**12. Rollerblade (Railway)**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Leg joint mobility/alignment, separation skills, carving on outside ski
- Key steps: Natural width stance; edge both skis by rolling both ankles and knees forward and inside; execute patiently at slow speed; leave clean carved tracks; find the sweet spot of the ski
- Image: `p026_01.png`

**13. Figure 8 Skating**
- Difficulty: Entry to Development
- Purpose: Coordinated body movements, movement from a working outside ski
- Key steps: Perform large figure-8 on variety of terrain using skating and poling; maintain alignment and upper body stability

**14. Hit-Hit-Hit**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Alignment/form, force/impulse, lateral mobility
- Key steps: During execution phase, lateral "hit" to both ski edges using both knees/ankles; optional pole touch per hit; maintain upper body stability throughout
- Image: `p028_01.png`

**15. Step-Step-Arc**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, alignment and form, linking/rhythm, centre of mass in line of travel
- Key steps: Two forward/uphill steps across hill off the downhill ski; on second step, steer ski into fall line and complete full arc; repeat

**16. Vrenni's Drill**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Pole use, coordinated movements, cognitive cues, anticipation/decision making
- Key steps: Lift right ski, perform 3 slalom arcs on left ski; put both skis down for 3 arcs; lift left ski for 3 arcs on right; repeat
- Image: `p030_01.png`

**17. One Ski Skiing**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Lateral/fore-aft/vertical movement adjustments, alignment and form, rhythm and linking
- Key steps: Perform variety of turn shapes at various speeds on one ski; use pole touch to enhance upper body stability; look forward and think ahead

**18. Inside Ski Turns**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Pole plant skills, coordinated movements, anticipation, upper body stability
- Key steps: At start of turn, lift the outside ski off snow; complete the turn on the inside ski; maintain alignment and link with rhythm

**19. Wide to Narrow to Wide to Natural**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Decision making, alignment and form, stability and mobility
- Key steps: Start with very wide stance; gradually bring to narrowest possible stance; shift to natural/athletic stance; exaggerate width changes multiple times

**20. Too Far**
- Difficulty: Entry to Performance
- Purpose: Forward/backward movement adjustment, stability with mobility
- Key steps: Rock as far forward as possible, then as far back as possible; reduce exaggeration gradually until finding stability through skeleton/musculature; recognise sweet spot of the ski

**21. Backwards Skiing (Pierre's Drill)**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Spatial awareness, decision making, coordinated movements
- Key steps: Face up the hill; perform linked parallel turns; safety is priority
- Image: `p034_01.png`

**22. Linked 360's**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Spatial awareness, coordinated movements
- Key steps: With adequate speed on smooth/consistent conditions, spin fully completed 360-degree circles; alternate direction every 3–4 rotations

**23. Spin Around**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Anticipation, coordinated movements, resistance training
- Key steps: Two-person drill; each holds end of linked bamboo poles; one skier goes downhill; as pole becomes taut, first skier offers resistance; creates revolving 360 down slope
- Image: `p037_01.png`

**24. Hockey Stop**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Separation/coiling, lower body mobility, pole plant, upper body rotational awareness
- Key steps: From standstill, build speed down fall line; turn both skis perpendicular to direction of travel; strongly set both edges; upper body faces fall line

**25. Children's Spiess**
- Difficulty: Entry level (without skis)
- Purpose: Lower leg turning, upper/lower body separation, pole touch/plant use
- Key steps: Without skis, perform small jumps turning feet perpendicular to line of travel; upper body and hips remain square to fall line; stabilise with pole plant
- Image: `p039_01.png`

**26. Spiess**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Pole plant (stability), upper/lower body separation, jumping with lower body using all joints
- Key steps: Small jump, turn skis perpendicular to line of travel; hips and shoulders face down the fall line; stabilise with pole touch/plant

**27. Edge to Edge**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, pole plant, lower body jumping/force/impulse application
- Key steps: Small jump with skis in slight wedge; land on inside edge of outside ski; repeat to other ski
- Image: `p040_01.png`

**28. Toes to Sky**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Vertical movement with legs, stability on outside ski
- Key steps: Start turn with both arms reaching high above head; as turn progresses, come down and touch outside of downhill boot with downhill hand

**29. Jump Turns**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Vertical movement, jumping with legs using all joints
- Key steps: Bring both skis off snow by jumping from a flexed position at end of turn; think ahead; move centre of mass in line of travel; maintain rhythm
- Image: `p043_01.png`

**30. Airplane**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Lateral movement, force application to outside ski, upper body stability
- Key steps: Arms out at shoulder level; outside arm reaches down the hill over outside ski; inside arm stays high creating a pinching sensation at waist over outside ski
- Image: `p044_01.png`

**31. Norwegian Pole Plant**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, upper/lower body separation/coiling, pole plant, force application to outside ski
- Key steps: In fall-line turns, both poles planted to outside of turn using both arms simultaneously; shoulders and hips face down the fall line; work with rhythm and pulse
- Image: `p045_01.png`

**32. Charleston**
- Difficulty: Entry to Development
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, lateral mobility, rhythm and linking, upper body stability
- Key steps: Variation of inside ski turns; fall-line version where skier quickly hops from inside ski to inside ski in a dance fashion; quiet arms, quiet upper body
- Image: `p046_01.png`

**33. Spaghetti Legs**
- Difficulty: Entry to Development
- Purpose: Lateral mobility, upper body stability, coordinated leg movements
- Key steps: Bring knees together (knock-kneed) then apart (bowlegged) to design an hourglass track; minimal spray indicates clean execution
- Image: `p047_01.png`

**34. Glenn's Drill**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Independent legs, alignment and mobility, force application to outside ski
- Key steps: In linked turns, step uphill ski out at top of turn in plow position; set new edge; balance and move body outside over new ski; drive both skis into fall line and complete turn
- Image: `p048_01.png`

**35. Power Plow**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Independent leg mobility, stability from outside ski, upper/lower body separation
- Key steps: In wedge position, alternate shifting weight from left to right ski; focus on stability on a well-edged ski through fall-line fully completed arcs

**36. Side Slip**
- Difficulty: Performance to High Performance
- Purpose: Left/right side symmetry, upper/lower body separation, lateral ankle/knee movement
- Key steps: Steer both skis across fall line; upper body faces line of travel with arms forward; release and set edges using ankles and knees
- Image: `p050_01.png`

**37. Pole Pinch**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Separation skills, aligned position over outside ski
- Key steps: Place full-length bamboo behind neck; rest arms and hands shoulder-width over pole; mobile lower body while maintaining upper body stability

**38. Draw a Line**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Upper/lower body separation, pole touch/plant, anticipation, rhythm and linking
- Key steps: Coach draws a line in corridor; skiers use line as focus point for turns; establish external focus and line awareness

**39. Tuck Turns**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Leg symmetry, upper body rotational stability, tuck position discipline
- Key steps: Long arc turns in tuck position; body position must not interfere with leg movement; direct skis with legs

**40. Banana Turn (Highest Mark)**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Upper/lower body separation (coiling), alignment over outside ski, carving on outside ski
- Key steps: From stop, straight run for speed; direct skis across fall line and uphill as far as possible; carve outside ski until stopped by hill

**41. Outside Boot Touch**
- Difficulty: Entry to Performance
- Purpose: Rhythmical coordinated movements, alignment on outside ski, upper/lower body separation
- Key steps: Without poles, reach with outside hand and touch downhill ski boot during turn; return to fully extended position between turns

**42. Outside Ski Only**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, stability on one ski, pole use, alignment
- Key steps: Lift inside ski off snow throughout entire turn; perform complete turn on outside ski only; anticipate required movements; maintain rhythm

**43. Roller Knees (Hands on Knees)**
- Difficulty: Development to Performance
- Purpose: Knee direction with hands, coordinated movements, alignment over outside ski
- Key steps: Without poles, place hands on downhill side of both knees; guide knees laterally into hill; at turn completion, extend legs and move hands to downhill side for new turn

**44. Two on a Bamboo**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Leg agility, upper body stability, separation, ski impulse across fall line
- Key steps: Two skiers each hold ends of two bamboo poles; both face downhill; downhill skier straight runs; uphill skier controls speed with heavy hockey stops

**45. Synchronized Skiing**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Coordinated movements, anticipation, rhythm and linking
- Key steps: In pairs or more, perform turns in various formations as one unit (side-by-side, V, behind each other, square, diamond)

**46. Chase**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Agility training, anticipation, cognitive engagement
- Key steps: Pair skiers and play chase; emphasis on figure-8 movements, skating, quick turns

**47. Thousand Turns**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Lower body agility/mobility, upper body stability, external focus
- Key steps: Coach picks a location; skiers compete to perform most turns in the distance to coach; model turns required

**48. Bump Skiing**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Anticipation, cognitive cues, line choice, pole use
- Key steps: Perform slalom or GS turns in bump terrain; maintain ski/snow contact; use markers for external cues

**49. All Conditions**
- Difficulty: Performance to High Performance
- Purpose: Versatility/agility, technical elements, speed management
- Key steps: Challenge skiers in heavy snow, windblown crust, powder, ice, chop; safety first

**50. All Terrain**
- Difficulty: Performance to High Performance
- Purpose: Upper body stability/lower body mobility, rhythm and linking, speed management
- Key steps: Challenge skiers in all terrain types: steep, flat, side hill, bumps, jumps, terrain garden, drop offs

**51. Follow the Leader**
- Difficulty: Entry to High Performance
- Purpose: Anticipation, versatility/agility
- Key steps: Follow coach or each other; pattern leader's line, speed, and turn shape; maximum three in a chain

**52. Shadow Drill**
- Difficulty: Performance to High Performance
- Purpose: Cognitive engagement, anticipation, line interpretation and speed management
- Key steps: Use environmental aids (shadows, features) to compel line and turn shape adjustments

**53. Snow Blade Drill**
- Difficulty: Development to High Performance
- Purpose: Versatility/agility, upper body stability/lower body mobility, speed management
- Key steps: Use short shape skis (snow blades) to promote versatility and adaptation

## 3. Progression Structure

The handbook uses a four-level LTAD-based system: Entry (U8–U12), Development (U14–U16), Performance (U18–U21), High Performance (U21+).

Eight skill categories classify every drill: Global Movements, Lateral Mobility, Fore-Aft Movements, Vertical Movement, Line/Anticipation/External Focus, Force/Impulse, Separation/Coiling/Rotational Stability, Coordination/Agility/Mobility.

**Checkpoint mapping:**

| Our checkpoint | Handbook level | Key drills |
|---|---|---|
| First Steps | Below Entry (handbook assumes skiing already established) | — |
| Basic Control | Entry Level (partial) | Children's Spiess, Turn Shape Course, Follow the Leader |
| Green Run Independence | Entry Level | Wide to Narrow, Too Far, Hands-All-Over, Thousand Steps |
| Blue Run Confidence | Entry to Development | Rollerblade, One Ski Skiing, Hoppsving, Step-Step-Arc |
| Rec: Smooth Blues | Development | Vrenni's Drill, Tuck Turns, Corridor Sets GS |
| Rec: Red Runs | Development to Performance | All Terrain, Bump Skiing, Glenn's Drill, Banana Turn |
| Moguls: Introduction | Development | Bump Skiing, Jump Turns, Hoppsving |
| Moguls: Rhythm | Performance | Thousand Turns, All Conditions, Spiess |
| Off-piste Fundamentals | Performance | All Conditions, All Terrain, Snow Blade Drill |
| Backcountry Touring | **Not covered** | — |
| Edge Mastery | Development to Performance | Rollerblade, Banana Turn, Outside Ski Only, Tuck Turns |
| Speed & Pressure | Performance to High Performance | Race Simulation, Hit-Hit-Hit, Norwegian Pole Plant, Side Slip |

> **Key observation:** The handbook does not cover snowplow/wedge at all — it assumes the athlete already skis. Our First Steps and Basic Control checkpoints have no handbook support and must be written from other sources.

## 4. Gaps & Improvements

### What the handbook has that our roadmap/exercises are missing

1. **Upper/lower body separation** — the handbook's single most repeated concept; never named in our roadmap
2. **Outside ski weighting** — the central biomechanical principle of every drill; our exercises never frame turns in terms of outside ski dominance
3. **Pole plant as a coordination trigger** — multiple dedicated drills; our roadmap mentions it once; `exercises.ts` has no pole-plant exercise
4. **Fore-aft movement as a distinct skill** — "Too Far", "Hip Lift", "Posture Exercise" are all devoted to this; our roadmap treats stance generally
5. **Vertical movement / extension-flexion** — "Hoppsving", "Toes to Sky", "Jump Turns", "Spiess" train this as the mechanism for turn initiation; our roadmap never mentions it
6. **Lateral mobility (independent legs)** — "Edge to Edge", "Glenn's Drill", "Spaghetti Legs", "Rollerblade"; our exercises address this only through off-snow lateral hops
7. **Line and anticipation (looking ahead)** — multiple dedicated drills; our roadmap mentions this only in a single tip
8. **Terrain/conditions diversity as deliberate training** — "All Terrain", "All Conditions"; our roadmap treats variable conditions as a situation to handle, not a training progression
9. **Tactical awareness** — line interpretation and rhythm from Development level; our Performance branch has none
10. **Mental/cognitive preparation** — Start and Finish Drills, Race Simulation; our roadmap has none

Drills entirely absent from our `exercises.ts`: Hoppsving, Rollerblade, Norwegian Pole Plant, Side Slip, One Ski Skiing, Tuck Turns, Banana Turn, Thousand Steps.

### What our roadmap conflicts with or contradicts

1. **`parallel-turn` step 4: "Rotate hips and shoulders in the direction of the turn"** — This is the exact fault the handbook's core principle and 30+ drills are designed to correct. **Must be changed.**
2. **"Carving" labelled advanced only** — The handbook introduces carving mechanics from Entry level; it is woven into basic technique, not a separate advanced discipline.
3. **"Short Radius Turns" missing key cues** — Omits upper body stability, pole plant as trigger, and vertical movement — the three mechanisms the handbook identifies for short-radius turns.
4. **"Blue Run Confidence" lacks technique specifics** — The handbook expects specific proficiencies at this level (outside ski completion, lateral mobility, pole coordination, vertical movement); our checkpoint is entirely situational.
5. **"Edge Mastery" says "resist over-rotating" without explaining how** — The handbook's mechanism is: use feet and legs to initiate; upper body stays stable through coiling/separation.

## 5. Recommended Roadmap Updates

### Priority Order

1. Fix `parallel-turn` step 4 — active misinformation contradicting the handbook's core principle
2. Add `hoppsving` exercise — most broadly useful drill, applicable from green runs onward
3. Add `side-slip` exercise — fundamental edge-awareness drill missing entirely
4. Add `norwegian-pole-plant` exercise — fills the pole-plant gap across the entire roadmap
5. Update Blue Run Confidence skills — weakest checkpoint for technical specifics
6. Add `rollerblade-drill` exercise — best drill for carving mechanics at intermediate level
7. Add `jump-turns` exercise — needed for mogul branch
8. Add `banana-turn` exercise — best drill for Performance/Edge Mastery checkpoint
9. Add `one-ski-skiing` exercise — advanced balance drill for performance branch
10. Add outside ski weighting language to Green Run Independence, Blue Run Confidence, Edge Mastery

**`rec-smooth-blues`** — Add skill: `"Can vary width of stance intentionally — wide for stability, narrow for agility"`

### New Exercises for `exercises.ts`

**`hoppsving`** — difficulty: intermediate, type: technique, duration: 20–40 min on groomed slope
Steps: adopt athletic stance with natural knee bend; perform small two-footed hops continuously around entire arc; stay stable over outside ski even as you hop off both; move off balls of feet, not heels; keep upper body still while legs hop through the arc.
Tips: use as warm-up drill; hops should feel like a controlled bounce not a jump; hopping back into the seat means hops are too large.
Muscles: quadriceps, calves, tibialis anterior, core, glutes.

**`rollerblade-drill`** — difficulty: intermediate, type: technique, duration: 30–60 min on groomed slope
Steps: stand with natural width of stance; roll both ankles and knees forward and to the inside simultaneously; allow edged skis to carve a long patient arc — do not rush; upper body remains stable and facing downhill — only legs move; look for two clean carved tracks (spray indicates insufficient edge angle).
Tips: think rolling ankles like inline skates; patience at slow speed lets you feel the ski arc; a bamboo pole held at shoulder height keeps the upper body still.
Muscles: adductors, tibialis anterior, peroneals, quadriceps, core.

**`norwegian-pole-plant`** — difficulty: advanced, type: technique, duration: 30–45 min on moderate to steep groomed terrain
Steps: begin fall-line parallel turns at moderate speed; as you complete each turn, reach both arms to the outside of the new turn simultaneously; plant both poles at the same time at a distance that pulls you laterally over the outside ski; hips and shoulders must face down the fall line throughout; use the pole plant as the trigger to release old edges and begin the new arc.
Tips: both poles should hit at exactly the same time — asymmetric planting indicates upper body rotation; add a small hop between turns to exaggerate the coiling sensation.
Muscles: core, hip rotators, forearms, deltoids, quadriceps.

**`side-slip`** — difficulty: intermediate, type: technique, duration: 20–30 min on moderate to steep groomed slope
Steps: stand across fall line with upper body facing slightly downhill and arms forward; release both edges by gently rolling knees away from hill; control the slide by rolling knees back into hill to set edges; repeat release and set in rhythm; try diagonal and forward/backward slips to build full edge awareness.
Tips: ankles and knees do all the work — do not use upper body; pole plant at moment of edge set helps stabilise; practise on both sides to identify left/right asymmetry.
Muscles: tibialis anterior, peroneals, quadriceps, adductors, core.

**`one-ski-skiing`** — difficulty: advanced, type: balance, duration: 20–40 min on groomed slope
Steps: perform linked fall-line turns on one ski; stay centred over the ball of the foot; use poles actively for balance and to trigger each turn; progress to varying turn shapes and steeper terrain; switch to the other ski and repeat.
Tips: look well ahead — reacting late is exaggerated on one ski; this drill reveals fore-aft and lateral alignment faults invisible on two skis.
Muscles: peroneals, tibialis anterior, glutes, core, quadriceps.

**`jump-turns`** — difficulty: advanced, type: technique, duration: 20–30 min on groomed or mogul terrain
Steps: complete a turn with skis across the fall line and knees bent in a strong flex; push explosively through both feet to lift both skis off the snow simultaneously; while airborne, redirect skis toward new fall line with a twist of the legs; land softly and immediately begin absorbing into next flex; maintain consistent rhythm.
Tips: upper body stays calm and facing downhill — only legs jump and redirect; pole plant just before each jump triggers the movement; start on gentle terrain.
Muscles: quadriceps, calves, hip flexors, core, glutes.

**`banana-turn`** — difficulty: advanced, type: technique, duration: 15–30 min on moderate to steep groomed slope
Steps: start from stationary position facing straight downhill; straight run to build speed — resist urge to turn too early; begin carving on outside ski directing both skis across fall line; coil upper body away from direction of travel keeping it facing downhill; continue carving outside ski uphill as far as possible until naturally stopped.
Tips: compete with yourself or partner on who leaves cleanest and highest arc; skidding means insufficient edge pressure or too much upper body rotation.
Muscles: adductors, quadriceps, hip rotators, core, tibialis anterior.

### Existing Exercises to Update

**`parallel-turn`** — Remove step 4 ("Rotate hips and shoulders in the direction of the turn"). Replace with: "Keep hips and shoulders facing downhill — let the feet and legs do the turning". Add tip: "If your upper body rotates into the turn, the skis will skid rather than carve".

**`carving`** — Update tip 2 from "Think 'tipping' not 'turning'" to "Think 'rolling the ankle and knee' to create edge angle — the ski's sidecut does the turning".

**`short-radius-turns`** — Add after step 2: "Keep upper body stable and facing downhill — only the legs rotate to create the short radius". Add tip: "If your shoulders are swinging, the turn is being forced rather than carved".
